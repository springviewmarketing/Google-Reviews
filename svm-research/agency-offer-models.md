# Paul Gough: the deep dive

## Who he is and why the story works

Started a physio clinic in 2007 in Hartlepool, which he repeatedly calls one of the poorest towns in the UK. He lists the obstacles himself: no doctor referrals, no money in the town, and a public "brainwashed to believe physio should be free".

He scaled it to four locations, over £60,000 a month, and more than 1,000 patient visits a month (Ep 686). Those are his figures, self-reported, unaudited.

The positioning line that carries the entire personal brand:

> "Not by being the best physio. I did it by becoming a world-class marketer of my physio business."

**Why this matters to you.** He is not selling marketing expertise. He is selling the proof that an ordinary clinician in a poor town beat a free state alternative. The clinic is the credential. Everything else is downstream of that one asset.

**Your equivalent asset does not exist yet, and that is the single biggest gap in your business.** You have no owned practice, no operator track record, and no origin story of that shape. What you have instead is photography skill and a niche. That is a weaker foundation than his and you should plan around it rather than pretend otherwise. Options are in the final section.

## The offer ladder, with real prices

| Rung | Product | Price | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Six or seven books | **Free** (physiofunnels.com/free) | Lead capture and authority in one move |
| 1 | Cash Pay Summit, 2 days | **$997**, anchored against $2,000, **only 20 seats** | Ascension event, sells from the room |
| 1b | Mastermind guest pass, 2 days | **$750** or 2 x $395 | Trial of the top tier |
| 2 | New Patient Accelerator | **$3,800** one payment, from $5,000, or 4 x $997 | Core programme, 10 months |
| 3 | Best Year Mastermind | **$12,900 per year** | High tier, application and phone interview to join |
| 4 | PhysioFunnels | **"less than $1,000 per month"**, **12-month minimum, 60 days' notice** | Recurring revenue, hybrid software and done-for-you |

Also in the catalogue: Planet Paul Marketing Academy, Business Growth School, Online Program Income Explosion, Telehealth Income Explosion, Effortless Selling System, Selling to Serve, Hassle-Free Staff.

**Read the ladder structurally and three things jump out.**

1. **The books are free, not cheap.** He is not making money on the front end at all. He is buying attention with his best asset and monetising at rungs 2 to 4.
2. **The recurring layer is the cheapest thing he sells.** Under $1,000 a month, but on a 12-month lock with 60 days' notice. That is where the predictable revenue lives, and it is priced to be an easy yes, not a considered purchase.
3. **The events are deliberately tiny.** Twenty seats. Twenty-five general admission. This is not a capacity constraint, it is a selling mechanic. Small rooms convert to high-ticket programmes at rates large rooms never do.

## The guarantee, dissected

This is the most copyable single thing in the research. The New Patient Accelerator carries what he calls a **Personal Profitability Guarantee**:

> If by module three you do not believe you will gain three additional patients to cover your investment, he refunds your money, less a 5% processing fee.

Four design decisions, all deliberate:

1. **It is belief-based, not results-based.** He never has to verify an outcome or argue about attribution. The test is the buyer's own confidence.
2. **It is time-boxed.** Module three, not month ten. The refund window closes long before the promise is due.
3. **It redenominates the price.** $3,800 stops being a course fee and becomes "three patients". The buyer now compares it to their own patient value, not to other courses. This is the whole trick.
4. **The 5% fee filters tyre-kickers** and covers card costs, at a level too small to look mean.

The headline promise sits alongside it: increase clinic profits by **50% to 250%** in 10 to 12 months, guaranteed. Note the range. The lower bound is easy to hit and defend; the upper bound does the marketing.

**Compare the aggression of NX Growth Partner's "10+ qualified patients in 90 days or you don't pay".** That is a stronger-sounding guarantee and a far more dangerous one to operate, because it is results-based and countable. Gough's version gets most of the risk-reversal benefit with almost none of the exposure. Copy his, not theirs.

## The $10K Workshop Method, and why it is the mechanic you actually want

His most recent book is *Teach And Get Rich: The $10K Workshop Method That Turns Physical Therapy Knowledge Into Real Profit*.

The premise: run one small-group educational workshop, turn it into $10,000, and fill the diary with cash-paying patients. He claims it has helped **200+ clinics add a predictable $10K+ in extra profit every month**. Self-reported, no methodology, treat as marketing.

PhysioFunnels bundles workshop-filling tools and quotes the outcome as **"$8-10K / £4-5K per month"**. Note he halves the figure in sterling, which is a quietly honest acknowledgement that UK clinics carry lower prices than US ones. Expect the same discount in your own modelling.

**This is the mechanic that answers your actual complaint.** A workshop is not content. It is an acquisition event that converts attention into booked high-value cases in a single sitting, on a known date, with a countable result. It is the difference between "your engagement is up" and "you have eleven new myopia management patients".

## The pricing philosophy

From Ep 016, his best pricing episode. His own clinic charged the equivalent of $150 a visit while local competitors charged around $60.

> "if you're in the pack, charging close to what everyone else is and the difference is only $5 or $10 per visit, then they will always go with the lowest... If you are $100 more expensive, they start to ask why you're so different."

**A small premium loses on price. A large premium changes the question.** That is the argument, and it applies to your own pricing more urgently than to your clients'. At £400 a month you are in the pack and you will be compared. At £2,000 a month the buyer has to ask what you know that the others do not.

Other lines worth keeping:

- "Your pricing isn't a math problem, it's a mirror." (Ep 720)
- On authority: "authority has nothing to do with credentials or experience, and it has everything to do with how you use your marketing to show people that you can achieve an outcome better than any other option they've got." (Authority Marketing, 2018)
- "Telling people you are friendly and experienced will not do this."

One claim to discard: "how people actually make decisions, and why it has nothing to do with cost in 80% of cases" (Ep 016). No source, and the number is doing no work that the argument does not already do.

## The sales insight most healthcare businesses miss

From a 2017 session where he put his own clinic manager on stage to be interrogated by other clinic owners:

> "For most Physiotherapy Businesses, the salesman is the referring DOCTOR. That's fine if you are happy to rely upon Doctor referrals, but if you are wanting to be successful without relying upon Doctors, then knowing how to sell your value is something you will need to get VERY good at."

His clinic runs a "Follow-Up Team", which he says plainly is a sales department. "Just because it's healthcare, doesn't mean that we are exempt from needing to sell the value of our services."

**Translated to optics: the salesman is NHS entitlement and high street footfall.** Take that away, or try to sell anything above a funded sight test, and the practice needs a sales function it almost certainly does not have. This is also where Progressive Dental makes its money, see below.

## The content engine

803 episodes between July 2016 and September 2026. Episode descriptions average around 1,450 characters, which means the show notes are themselves substantial sales copy.

Two current formats, and the structure is instructive:

- **"Coffee with Goughy"** - long, 55 to 75 minutes, live Q&A answering clinic owners' real problems. Episode titles are literally the owner's question in their own words: "My clinic depends on me for everything. What am I doing wrong?", "Why does charging more still feel wrong even when I get results?", "I've Been Searching For A PT For 18 Months!"
- **"Paul on Fire"** - short, 6 to 18 minutes, punchy single-argument opinion pieces.

**Why the Q&A format is the engine.** One live session simultaneously produces content, surfaces objections in the buyer's own language, demonstrates expertise in public, and generates the titles. He is not writing content, he is recording work.

**The drift worth noticing.** His 2026 output is dominated by staff, hiring and leadership, not marketing. He has moved up the value chain from "get more patients" to "run the business properly". That is the natural end point of this model and it tells you where the money eventually sits.

He also runs invitation-only mastermind events **at his own clinic**, quarterly, and puts his own staff on stage. The operating business is simultaneously the proof, the venue and the content. Ruthlessly efficient.

## The lineage

He has worked with **Dan Kennedy** for ten years and has brought him on stage at his own events, alongside Kevin O'Leary, Daymond John and James Lawrence.

**This is the most useful structural fact in the whole report.** Paul Gough did not invent his model. It is the standard Dan Kennedy info-marketing playbook, applied to one professional niche: free book to capture the lead, paid small-room event to ascend, high-ticket programme, mastermind, recurring software layer. Because it is a template, you can copy it deliberately rather than reverse-engineering it from his behaviour.

# Progressive Dental: the offer architecture worth stealing

Founded 2009 by Bart Knellinger, after his father, a dentist, asked how to attract more patients needing advanced procedures.

**What makes it interesting is not the marketing. It is what they attached to it.**

They focus almost entirely on **full-arch dental implants**, a procedure worth roughly $20,000 to $50,000 a case. Then they built **The Closing Institute**, a sales training arm with a mentorship programme, a Full-Arch Boot Camp and a Full-Arch Growth Conference, which trains the practice's own team to pre-qualify, handle and close the leads the agency generates.

Their stated reason, and it is the whole thesis:

> Clients have been able to 10x their ROI on marketing spend **because they train their teams to pre-qualify, handle and close the leads** generated for them.

Their conference is marketed on a blueprint to "close 10, 20, 30 arches monthly".

**Three lessons.**

1. **Pick the high-ticket procedure, not the practice.** They do not sell "dental marketing". They sell full-arch case volume. The niche inside the niche is what lets them charge.
2. **Sell the conversion layer, not just the leads.** Leads a practice cannot close are a refund request waiting to happen. Selling the closing system protects your results and doubles your revenue per client.
3. **The training is a second product with near-zero marginal cost.** Bootcamps and conferences monetise the same expertise again.

They publish no pricing and no guarantee, so I cannot tell you what they charge. Anyone claiming otherwise is guessing.

# Hormozi: the framework you named

Included only as the scaffold to hang the above on, since you referenced it directly.

**The value equation:**

> Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice)

Raise the top, lower the bottom. A grand slam offer is one so far outside the comparison set that price shopping stops working.

**Gym Launch as the worked example**, since it is the closest thing to "Hormozi if all he did was one wellbeing niche". He and Leila initially flew to individual gyms and ran the campaigns in person. When he tried to quit, a gym owner asked to be taught the system instead, which became the licensing business. Reported structure: roughly $16,000 to $18,000 upfront early on, later enrolment fees past $10,000 with monthly licensing around $1,500 to $2,000. Reported to have reached around $17m revenue; 66% was sold in 2021 for $46.2m.

**The pattern to notice.** Done-for-you was the prototype, not the business. He used it to prove the system, then sold the system. Paul Gough did exactly the same thing from the other direction. Both men stopped delivering the marketing.

Figures here are from secondary reporting and summaries, not audited accounts. Treat them as directionally useful only.

# Translating this into an offer for Spring View Marketing

## The core problem with your current shape

You sell social media and blogs to independent opticians. That is a **cost line** on their P&L with no countable output. It gets cut first in a bad quarter, it invites price comparison, and it caps at a few hundred pounds a month because that is what "social media" is worth in the buyer's head.

Every business in this report escaped that by attaching themselves to **a specific high-value service and a countable outcome**.

## The high-ticket lines that exist inside a UK optical practice

This is where your grand slam offer has to live:

- **Myopia management** - roughly £500 to £1,500 a year per child, multi-year, recurring, clinically urgent, and parents actively search for it
- **Dry eye clinics and IPL** - roughly £400 to £800 a course, repeat treatments
- **Premium and specialist lenses** - £500 to £900 a pair, pure margin above the NHS baseline
- **Specialist contact lenses** - ortho-k, sclerals, high value and sticky
- **Private enhanced eye exams and OCT** - the upsell that funds everything else

Myopia management is the strongest candidate. It is the full-arch implant of optics: high value, recurring, parent-driven, emotionally urgent, underserved by the multiples, and something an independent can credibly own locally.

## The proposed offer

**Stop selling social media. Sell filled myopia management clinics.**

The mechanic, lifted directly from the $10K Workshop Method and Progressive Dental's closing layer:

1. **The event.** A parents' evening at the practice on children's eye health and myopia. Fifteen to twenty-five parents in the room, from a local campaign you run.
2. **Your photography does the work it is actually good for.** Real footage of the practice, the equipment, the optometrist and the children's testing process, used to fill the room. This is the one place your differentiator directly drives a countable outcome rather than a nicer grid.
3. **The conversion layer.** You train the practice team on the pre-qualification call, the room, the follow-up sequence and the objection handling. This is Progressive Dental's lesson and it is what protects your result.
4. **The recurring layer.** Ongoing local content, email recall, review generation and Google Business Profile, priced as the retention wrapper around the events.

**Worked economics, with the assumptions stated so you can argue with them.** Twenty parents attend, eight convert to a £1,000-a-year programme with a typical three-year horizon. That is £8,000 in year one and up to £24,000 of lifetime value from a single evening. These are illustrative numbers built from the price ranges above, not measured results. You would need to prove them on one practice before putting them in any pitch.

Against that, £1,500 to £3,000 a month is a rounding error, which is exactly the position Gough engineers with his three-patients framing.

## The guarantee to use

Copy Gough's structure, not NX Growth Partner's:

> If, by the end of the second event, you do not believe the programme will pay for itself in enrolled patients, I will refund the fee, less costs already committed to advertising.

Belief-based, time-boxed, and denominated in patients rather than pounds. It gives you the risk reversal without exposing you to a countable promise you cannot control.

**Do not** offer "X patients or you don't pay" until you have run the model enough times to know your conversion rate cold. It is the guarantee most likely to bankrupt you.

## Your missing asset, and three ways to get it

Gough has an operating clinic. Progressive Dental had a dentist father. You have neither, and a done-for-you agency with no operator story is the weakest position in this report.

Realistic routes, in order of how fast they work:

1. **Borrow it.** Run the first myopia evening free for one practice, on the explicit condition that you get the numbers and the right to publish them. One documented result beats any amount of positioning.
2. **Build it in public.** You have no clinic, but you do have a business being built from nothing in an unglamorous niche. That is the story Gough tells, and he was in Hartlepool. Start documenting now, before the results exist, because the archive is what compounds.
3. **Partner into it.** Co-brand with an existing myopia management or dry eye specialist who has the clinical credibility you lack.

# The £100k a month question, answered honestly

You asked for this specifically, so here is the arithmetic rather than encouragement.

**The market.** The UK opticians industry is around £5.8bn across roughly 5,547 businesses. Independents hold about 25% of the market by value, down from 35% a decade ago. Specsavers alone is 42%, Boots around 11% with about 550 practices, Vision Express around 9% with 395 stores. Realistically you are selling into a serviceable pool in the low thousands of practices, and it is shrinking as a share.

**What £100k a month requires:**

| Price per client per month | Clients needed | Share of the independent pool |
|---|---|---|
| £500 | 200 | Not achievable as a service business |
| £1,000 | 100 | Implausible for a photography-led model |
| £2,500 | 40 | Hard, needs a team of roughly 6 to 10 |
| £5,000 | 20 | Plausible only on a high-ticket outcome offer |
| £10,000 | 10 | Requires multi-site or small-chain clients |

**Three honest conclusions.**

1. **At your current price point the goal is arithmetically impossible.** Two hundred optician clients do not exist for you, and you could not serve them if they did.
2. **Photography-led delivery does not scale linearly.** Every client needs someone physically in the practice. That is your moat and your ceiling simultaneously. Past roughly fifteen to twenty clients you are hiring photographers, at which point you are running an operations business, not a marketing one.
3. **Nobody in this report got there by delivering marketing.** Gough sells programmes and software. Hormozi sold a licence. Progressive Dental sells training alongside the agency. The margin is in the leveraged layer, not the labour.

**The realistic path, in stages:**

- **Stage 1, £10k a month.** Six to eight clients at £1,250 to £1,500 on the event-led offer. Entirely you. This is the proving ground and the only stage where the numbers matter more than the model.
- **Stage 2, £30k a month.** Twelve to fifteen clients at £2,000 to £2,500, one employee, event mechanic productised and repeatable.
- **Stage 3, £60k a month.** Twenty clients at £3,000, plus a paid training product sold to practices you do not serve. This is where you stop trading hours.
- **Stage 4, £100k a month.** Requires a leveraged layer: a workshop-in-a-box programme, a paid annual event for independent opticians, or a software or recall layer on a twelve-month contract. **Assume this stage is three to five years out and requires you to become a teacher and a recruiter, not a photographer.**

If you are not willing to make that last shift, a very good outcome is £30k to £50k a month with a small team, which is an excellent business and worth saying out loud rather than treating as failure.

# What to steal, in order

1. **Change what you sell from social media to filled clinics for one named high-ticket service.** Myopia management first.
2. **Adopt the workshop or practice-event as your core acquisition mechanic.** It is countable, it has a date, and it produces a number you can put in a case study.
3. **Copy the belief-based, time-boxed guarantee** denominated in patients rather than money. Avoid results-based guarantees until your conversion rate is proven.
4. **Sell the conversion layer, not just the leads.** Train the practice team on the call, the room and the follow-up. This is what makes your results survive contact with a practice that cannot close.
5. **Give the book away.** Gough's front end is free and it is the engine of everything. Write the short, specific book for independent optician owners and never charge for it.
6. **Run the Q&A format.** Answer real owner questions live, title each episode with the question in their words, and let one recording produce the content, the objection research and the authority at once.
7. **Price to leave the pack.** At £400 you are compared. At £2,000 you are questioned, which is a better conversation. Move in one jump, not in increments.
8. **Put the recurring layer on a twelve-month term.** Gough uses twelve months plus sixty days' notice. Molly Cahill uses three months. The difference is the difference between a business and a freelance gig.
9. **Build the small room.** Twenty seats, application required, a few funded places for people you want as case studies. Small rooms sell high-ticket programmes.
10. **Start documenting the build now, before the results exist.** The archive is the asset, and it is the only one of these ten you cannot buy later.
